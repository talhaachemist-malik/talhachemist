Project 1 · MSc thesis ## Can TiO₂ turn ZnS into a sunlight photocatalyst? My thesis made zinc sulphide nanoparticles, combined them with TiO₂, and tested whether the composites could break down an organic dye in sunlight. Supervisor: Dr Waseeq Ahmad Siddiqui, University of Sargodha. - ZnS + TiO₂ - UV-Vis - FTIR - SEM - Photocatalysis ### *Method*From salt to composite 1. **ZnS**Zinc acetate with TBAC capping agent, H₂S bubbled through 2. **TiO₂ sol**TTIP in ethanol and nitric acid, 70 °C 3. **Composites**1, 3, 5, 7 and 10 % TiO₂ added during ZnS formation 4. **Characterise**UV-Vis, FTIR and SEM 5. **Test**Murexide dye, 100 minutes in sunlight ### *Results*Spectra and images Switch between pure ZnS and the 5 % ZnS/TiO₂ composite. Click any figure to enlarge it. **UV-Vis.** Absorption between 250 and 300 nm, nothing in the visible range. **FTIR.** KBr pellet, 400–4000 cm⁻¹, Zn–S region marked. **SEM.** Aggregated particles. SEM alone cannot size them. **Murexide test.** Six spectra, 0 to 100 minutes. The dye band stays where it was. **Result: no measurable degradation** Neither pure ZnS nor the 5 % composite broke down murexide (5 mM, natural sunlight at UV index 4). I report it as a negative result. ZnS and TiO₂ absorb mainly in the UV, and sunlight at UV index 4 delivers little of it. - Use a controlled UV lamp, with a dye-only control and a dark-adsorption step - Test the other TiO₂ ratios already prepared - Confirm composite formation with XRD and TEM, and calculate the band gap from Tauc plots These open questions (band alignment, visible-light response, proof of a true heterostructure) are the ones I want to take on in a master's project. Project 2 · Archaeometry ## What were Harappan pots made of? Three pottery sherds from Harappa, excavated in 1926 and kept in the Harappa Museum, Sahiwal. I used five analytical methods to read their composition, minerals and surfaces. - 3 sherds - XRF + PXRF - XRD - FTIR - SEM-EDX **Composition**Rich in silica and alumina: SiO₂ 56–60 %, Al₂O₃ about 19 %, Fe₂O₃ 6.6–7.2 %. **Minerals**XRD shows quartz and feldspar together with clay minerals. **Organics**Aliphatic C–H bands in FTIR point to organic residues. **Surfaces**SEM shows sintered surfaces, weathering cracks and traces of plant temper. ### *Samples*The sherds Sherds as received, then cut into small pieces for SEM and ground to powder for XRF, XRD and FTIR.
